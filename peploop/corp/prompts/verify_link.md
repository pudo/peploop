You check one statement before it is written to Wikidata: that a public body
controls another, as its owner or as the body it belongs to, or what kind of
body the other is. You are given the bodies and the passage from a
Wikipedia article that was read as the evidence.

A link is one of two properties, and you are told which:

- `owned by`: the public body holds the other. The passage supports it if
  it says, in its own words, that the public body owns or controls the
  other, holds all or most of its shares (said in words: a percentage or a
  list of the largest shareholders alone says nothing about control), is its
  parent company, or carries or sponsors it as its legal carrier.
- `parent organization`: the other belongs to the public body. The passage
  supports it if it says that the other is part of it, subordinate to it,
  one of its own institutions (alone or jointly with other public bodies),
  or falls under its responsibility or authority in the administration. A
  state legal person (an agency, a fund, an institute, a social insurer)
  that its minister supervises by statute is subordinate to that ministry,
  and so is a state enterprise or organisation whose rights as founder in
  law a ministry exercises.

Being one of a state's or a region's institutes, an institution "of" it, or
an organisational or budgetary unit of the state, supports a parent link to
the ministry or government named, never ownership by the state or region
itself. A body that several states or regions set up and carry together,
none of them leading, is owned by none of them. When the public body is a state and you are
told the passage names it as one of its ministries, its government or its
treasury, a holding by that ministry, government or treasury is the state's
own.

It supports neither when it only says that the public body founded or set
up the other (founding is not owning); that it funds the other, gives it
money, grants or a budget; that the other works at the level of that state
or region, or on its commission; that the public body supervises, regulates
or oversees it while the other is independent of it (an independent
regulator, a private association, a federation); that a treaty or law
between public bodies sets up its task; that the public body holds a
minority stake beside other owners; that it chairs a body of it; or when the
passage is about a different body.

When you are given a claim instead of a public body -- that the other is a
state-owned enterprise -- the passage supports it if it says so in words: the
body is state-owned or publicly owned, or a state, a subdivision of one or a
public body of either owns, controls or runs it as above. A share figure or
a ranking of shareholders is not such a statement.

When the claim is a class -- a regulator, a public broadcaster, a central
bank -- you are also given the opening of the body's article, and the two
together support it if they say the body is one. A regulator sets binding
rules for a sector or market, licenses it, or supervises the firms in it. A
body that keeps a register, grants patents or trademarks, inspects,
audits, advises, investigates, watches a budget, fights doping, or manages a
resource or infrastructure is not a regulator for that. A public broadcaster
broadcasts radio or television as a public service; a central bank is a
country's central or national bank.

Answer `supports` true or false, and give the reason in one short sentence.
