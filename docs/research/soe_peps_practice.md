# SOE PEPs: regulators and industry practice on the edge cases

Desk research, 2026-10-17, on the 22 edge cases in `docs/soe_peps.md`.
[V] the primary text was read; [S] a secondary source (reseller page,
law-firm note, snippet); [U] unverified. FATF 2013, Wolfsberg 2017, FCA
FG17/6 and FG25/3 and the EBA Risk Factor Guidelines could only be read
through secondary sources. National lists of prominent public functions were
left out on purpose.

## Strongest findings

1. AMLR (vii) is a control test, not an ownership test: "enterprises
   controlled under any of the relationships listed in Article 22 of Directive
   2013/34/EU" [V]. "Any" arguably includes Art. 22(2): dominant influence,
   unified management.
2. Art. 22 controls are majority of votes, the right to appoint a board
   majority, dominant influence under contract or articles, and control by
   agreement. They count through subsidiaries (22(3)); in German law § 290(2)–(3)
   HGB [V].
3. The OECD SOE Guidelines (2024) define an SOE by "ownership or control".
   Statutory corporations count when their activities are "of an economic
   nature". A minority counts with "decisive influence" (veto, appointment of
   the board majority or the CEO, a golden share by substance). Regulation
   does not count [V].
4. BaFin AuA (July 2025, § 5.4) says "As a rule, only functions at an
   international, European or national level should be taken into account.
   Regional functions may be relevant in case of federal structures… In
   principle, municipal functions are not included." [V] It gives no SOE
   threshold. The AMLR overrides this from 10 July 2027 for regional and
   local control of medium and large undertakings.
5. UK (HMRC AMLG 16.6): covers "only… 'for profit' enterprises where the
   state has ownership of greater than 50% or where information reasonably
   available points to the state having control" [V]. *Hajiyeva v NCA* [2020]
   EWCA Civ 108 held a state bank's chairman to be a PEP [S].
6. Former PEPs (AMLR Art. 45(2)): measures apply "until such time as the
   risks… no longer exist, but in any case for not less than 12 months" [V].
7. Vendors are narrower than the AMLR and mostly opaque. Dow Jones requires
   ≥50 % *direct* central-government ownership, national scope, a "key
   industry", and excludes subsidiaries [S]. ComplyAdvantage has Class 3
   (national and regional SOEs) and Class 4 (local SOEs), with no threshold
   [V]. LexisNexis covers "majority or minority" government ownership [S].
8. Nothing anywhere on employee representatives, Prokuristen, broadcasting
   councils or temporary state ownership.
9. Size: § 267 HGB sets small at ≤ €7.5m balance sheet, ≤ €15m turnover,
   ≤ 50 employees, two of three criteria on two consecutive balance-sheet
   dates. Medium or large means exceeding two of the three [V].
10. Art. 2(2) excludes middle-ranking officials [V]. The UK draws the line at
    Permanent or Deputy Permanent Secretary [V].

## Recommended rule per case

1. **Institutions:** in the SOE limb when organised as a company (GmbH, AG,
   gGmbH) or as a public-law body with economic activity. Purely sovereign or
   cultural bodies are not.
2. **Public-law traders:** a controlled undertaking when a public authority
   sets the statute or appoints the governing body; tier of that authority.
3. **Public broadcasters:** out of the SOE limb unless public authorities
   appoint a majority of a governing body (Staatsferne).
4. **Non-profit state companies:** in. The AMLR has no profit test; the
   size threshold covers small ones.
5. **Foundations:** controlled when a public authority appoints a majority
   of the decisive body or holds veto or dissolution rights.
6. **Landesbetriebe:** out of the SOE limb. They have no legal personality,
   and their heads are officials.
7. **Agency heads:** not SOE PEPs; another limb or a national designation.
8. **Joint public control:** public owners together hold a majority of votes
   or seats. Tier of the largest public holder (federation over Land over
   municipality on ties). Flag it as joint.
9. **Minority with dominant influence:** in when the state is the largest
   shareholder with a de facto AGM majority, or holds statutory veto or
   appointment rights. A plain financial stake (Commerzbank 12 %) is out.
10. **Chains:** control at every link. A link without control breaks the
    chain. Percentages are never multiplied.
11. **State and municipal:** the federation is "state"; Länder and
    municipalities are regional or local, with the size test unless the
    federation is in the controlling bloc.
12. **Temporary ownership:** from the date control is acquired to the date it
    is lost; rescues count while they last.
13. **Size:** the undertaking, or the group it heads, exceeds two of the
    three § 267(1) HGB limits in its latest accounts.
14. **Municipal:** in above the size threshold, on an AMLR-2027 basis.
15. **Bodies:** those with legal power to appoint management or approve
    strategy (Vorstand, Geschäftsführung, Aufsichtsrat, Verwaltungsrat,
    Stiftungsrat). Purely advisory Beiräte and assemblies are out.
16. **Employee representatives:** in, as members; tag the seat.
17. **Management:** statutory body members only; not Prokuristen, division
    heads or extended management.
18. **Subsidiaries:** directly controlled ones that are medium or large
    themselves; deeper levels only when large.
19. **Rotating and ex officio chairs:** the seat with its dates, beside the
    political office.
20. **Former members:** the position with an end date; a current PEP for 12
    months, then former.
21. **Group and entity:** the parent always; subsidiaries per 18.
22. **Foreign:** the same control test for foreign states; a domestic group's
    foreign subsidiaries per 18.
