# Worklists and review-by-hand on Wikidata

Evidence gathered 2026-10-18 for the mass-editing proposal discussion (Ainali
asked for per-edit human review, "like the old style Wikidata games").

## Where it worked

- **Mix'n'match**: thousands of catalogues (5,800+ per Meta), matched by
  volunteers to items. Each decision is binary (same entity or not); catalogue
  pages show match progress. No aggregate study of accuracy or throughput.
- **Wikidata Game / Distributed Game** (Magnus Manske): one yes/no question per
  screen, edits tagged "Distributed Game" and attributed to whoever clicks. No
  published evaluation of quality; the MediaWiki page's "generally high" is
  unsourced.
- **Android Suggested Edits** (Wikidata descriptions): revert rates 2-5.5%
  (2017-18), near 0% after pausing low-quality users (2019). The apps team
  later noted that patrolling these edits "has been challenging on Commons and
  Wikidata" (2021 roadmap). Machine-suggested descriptions that a human
  accepted were reverted 2.3% of the time vs 15% for unassisted edits (small
  sample).

Common shape: one narrow, binary decision per screen, on data that needs no
modelling choice.

## Where it did not

- **Primary Sources tool** (Freebase migration): by 27 Sept 2019, 528,000 of
  10 million Freebase statements had been approved or rejected, about 5% in
  four years (Marco Fossati, wikidata-l). The tool was abandoned.
- **EveryPolitician** (mySociety, paused June 2019): moved its work into
  WikiProject every politician. Its Wikimedia grant final report: "it's much
  much harder to spot inconsistencies" item by item than in aggregate;
  Position History reports, not Listeria lists, were what worked; 39 countries
  reached its two-star level, out of 65 worked on. The pause post cites
  maintenance being "hugely time consuming, complex" and data "always steadily
  going out of date".
- **PoliLoom** (OpenSanctions): per-claim accept/reject of model extractions
  from Wikipedia. Tens of thousands of euros; few regular reviewers; accepted
  edits inconsistent, especially office-to-position matching; each method
  change needs a tool change and deployment and does not reach accepted edits.

## Reading

Worklists work for binary decisions with no modelling content. Matching an
office to the right position item, with dates and jurisdiction, is a modelling
decision; reviewers disagree on it, and consistency is what is lost. The
WikiProject's own predecessor found consistency is visible only in aggregate.
